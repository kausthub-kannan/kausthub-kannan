# Home

Welcome to the philosophical and Indic deep-dive section of my blog, **Vedanta Deepam**. Here I jot down my analysis and research on the Sruti, Smriti, Itihasa, Agama and Purana texts. Though these are the sacred texts of Sanatana Dharma, I have tried to take in their essence as the Purvacharyas of the Sri Vaishnava Siddhanta understood it, while viewing it through a modern lens.

The Sastras are of high value, yet difficult to understand as they are. Most Western and modern readings of the Vedas and their supporting texts are made from a Western viewpoint, though the texts themselves were taught and developed in the East. This matters because the Sastras make more sense when viewed from the right perspective. Vedanta, Itihasa and the other texts rest on core concepts: Karma; the cycle of birth and death, called Samsara; and liberation from this cycle, called Moksha. If the same Sastras are read without accepting Karma, Samsara and Moksha, they will naturally sound far-fetched and unconvincing.

This leads to the famous question of Science and the Vedas: whether they are the same, superset and subset, or contradictory. The real issue is not deciding which relationship they share, but that comparing them directly is misguided in the first place. In scientific terms, it is like applying the principles of classical physics to the quantum world, or vice versa, which obviously doesn’t work. Science deals with the material world, while the Vedas deal with the metaphysical. Trying to prove the existence of the Atman through science is like trying to explain the dual nature of light using classical physics.

With these views in mind, I have jotted down my daily readings and understanding of the Sastras, within my limits. This blog may not be the source of all truth, and I do not claim absolute correctness about the Sastras, given my own limitations and continuing study. Its aim is to move toward teaching the Vedantic Sastras along the path laid out by the Vishishtadvaita scholars. Because of the nature of the Vedas, the blog also touches the core points of other Indic philosophies and theologies, exploring sectarian perspectives too.
